Transfer limits

This node's line

These cap what this node's swarm traffic costs your line. They apply to everyone, friends included — they describe your pipe, not who deserves what. Leave a field empty to use the value from madshare.toml; enter 0 for unlimited.

Member budget

The caps above describe your pipe. These describe who may spend it: they bound what nodes you have not friended — members of your community, guests, a device carrying a token — may cost you. Direct friends bypass all four, because a friend is a decision you made and should not queue behind the ones the graph let in. Empty means the value from madshare.toml; 0 is unlimited.

What do the member limits do?

A node over its budget is told 429 busy before we even look up what it asked for — so the refusal says nothing about whether we hold the file. A healthy fetcher takes that as “ask another holder” and goes elsewhere; one with no other holder waits the congestion out. Being unable to serve right now is honest information, not an error.

The together limits are the ones that actually bound harm. A per-node limit is what a flood of fresh identities defeats — each forged key simply buys another quota — while the class ceiling is one number they all share. The per-node halves are fairness within that ceiling: one member cannot take the whole budget.

Concurrency is worth setting even if the rates stay open: a fetching node opens parallel requests by design, so it is transfers, not bytes, that one member most easily multiplies.

How big should the rate caps be?

Size them against your uplink, not the download speed your ISP quotes: KiB/s ≈ Mbit/s × 128. Leaving about a quarter of the uplink free is a good starting point — so a 20 Mbit uplink (≈2560 KiB/s) suggests roughly 1900.

Do not set them small. One listener streaming CD-rate FLAC needs ~125 KiB/s continuously, and every concurrent transfer shares the same bucket. Below that, this node does not merely get slower: fetching peers time it out, the swarm de-ranks it and goes elsewhere, and the bytes you already sent are wasted. If your line cannot spare ~256 KiB/s, switching seeding off on Settings is the honest setting rather than a starvation rate.

The download cap also slows anything you are listening to from the network, since the player reads from the same transfer.
